Interface Safe-to-Mate (Flight Vehicle)

This harness carries solar array power. Get an ISTM check wrong here and the failure mode isn't abstract — it's the array shorting its own output the moment sunlight hits it.

What You're Verifying

This harness carries the deployed solar array's output (Lesson 1.3's Capstone made EPS integration mandatory for exactly this reason) and its temperature sensor signal from the array to the Power Distribution Unit (PDU). A wiring harness has no internal component to check — there is no schematic in the CSTM sense. What you verify instead is that the harness matches its intended pin map: the right pin at one end lands on the right pin at the other, and no two different circuits are shorted together anywhere along the run.

Why ARRAY+ to ARRAY− Is the Check That Matters Most

Every pin pair in this harness should be isolated from every other pin pair — that is the general ISTM rule. But one specific pair carries the highest consequence: ARRAY+ to ARRAY−. A solar array under illumination is a live power source the moment sunlight reaches it, whether or not anything downstream is powered on. A short between these two pins, undetected before deployment, becomes a short across the array itself on orbit — with no technician able to walk out and unplug anything.

Exercise — Predict the ISTM Results

Draft your answer, then reveal a model answer to self-check.
Using Fig. 2.3-C, state the expected result for (1) continuity on each individual wire end-to-end, and (2) isolation between ARRAY+ and ARRAY− specifically.
MODEL ANSWER

Continuity: Each of the five wires (ARRAY+, ARRAY−, TEMP+, TEMP−, CASE) should read very low resistance from its P1 pin to its matching P2 pin, confirming the harness is wired straight through with no swapped or broken conductors.

ARRAY+ / ARRAY− isolation: Must read OPEN. Unlike a broken wire, which just disables that one circuit, a short between these two pins creates a direct path across the solar array's own output — a defect that only becomes visible once the array is illuminated on orbit, when it is far too late to fix.

  • Did you check continuity on all five wires, not just the array pair?
  • Did you explain the consequence, not just state "open"?

Scenario — A Low Reading Between Array Pins

You are running the isolation check across every pin pair per the ISTM procedure.
ARRAY+ to ARRAY− reads 15 Ω — not zero, but far from the open circuit this pair requires. Every other pin pair reads open as expected. What is your assessment?
KEY POINT

This is the same reasoning that made EPS a mandatory subsystem in the Part 1 Capstone: a defect that only shows up after deployment, on a subsystem you can't isolate or power off, has to be caught here — on the ground, before mating — or not at all.
