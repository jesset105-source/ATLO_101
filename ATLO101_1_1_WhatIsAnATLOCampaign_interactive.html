What is an ATLO Campaign?

Every spacecraft that reaches orbit passes through the same gate first: Assembly, Test, and Launch Operations. ATLO is where a collection of engineered components becomes one vehicle proven ready to fly.

What ATLO Actually Is

ATLO -- Assembly, Test, and Launch Operations -- is the phase of a spacecraft program where individually built components and subsystems come together into a single integrated vehicle, get proven to work correctly, and get certified ready for launch. It sits downstream of design and fabrication and upstream of launch itself -- the last major engineering phase before the spacecraft leaves the ground for good.

Nothing about ATLO is informal. Every operation performed on the spacecraft during this phase follows a documented, approved procedure, and every result is recorded. That discipline exists because ATLO is the last point where a problem is a fix instead of a mission loss.

The Two Halves of an ATLO Campaign

This course mirrors the actual structure of a campaign in two overarching phases. Planning comes first -- building the schedule, defining roles and decision authority, writing and validating procedures, and defining what "passing" a test actually means before any hardware is touched. Execution comes second -- receiving hardware, integrating it electrically and mechanically, running functional and environmental tests, working anomalies, and ultimately clearing the vehicle for shipment at a Pre-Shipment Review.

A campaign that skips planning does not move faster -- it moves blind. Every hour spent building a realistic schedule and a validated procedure before touching flight hardware is an hour that does not get spent chasing an anomaly that better preparation would have caught.

NPR 7120.5 Awareness

NPR 7120.5 -- NASA's Space Flight Program and Project Management Requirements -- governs how spaceflight programs are planned, reviewed, and approved at the agency level, including the major milestone reviews an ATLO campaign feeds into: Launch Readiness Reviews and Pre-Shipment Reviews among them. You are not expected to memorize this document. You are expected to know it exists, know roughly what it governs, and recognize when a campaign decision is being made in reference to it -- that awareness is what lets you understand why certain reviews and gates are non-negotiable.

KEY POINT

ATLO is not "testing at the end." It is a full engineering campaign with its own planning, its own schedule, its own roles, and its own discipline. Treat it that way from your first day, and the rest of this course will make sense as one connected picture instead of a list of unrelated topics.
