Anomaly Reporting & Disposition

Calling a hold is the easy part. This lesson is about what happens next — the actual document that turns "something looked wrong" into a tracked, resolved, and closed record.

The Discrepancy Report (DR)

Every anomaly that stops a procedure — a value outside expected range, a mechanical fit that doesn't match the drawing, a component that fails a CSTM check — gets documented in a Discrepancy Report. NPR 8735.2, NASA's Hardware Quality Assurance Requirements, governs this process: how non-conformances are documented, investigated, dispositioned, and closed. You are not expected to memorize the document, only to recognize that the DR process you are about to practice is the same one it defines.

The Four Disposition Categories

Every DR ends with a disposition — a formal decision about what happens to the hardware:

  • Use-As-Is: the deviation from the drawing or spec is accepted without any physical change, because engineering analysis shows it does not affect form, fit, or function
  • Repair: the defect is corrected by an approved method that does not fully restore the original drawing configuration, but makes the hardware acceptable for use
  • Rework: the hardware is brought back into full compliance with the original drawing or specification
  • Scrap: the hardware cannot be made acceptable and is removed from use entirely

Exercise — Write a Discrepancy Report

Draft your DR, then reveal a model answer to self-check.
During post-install inspection, a structural bracket bolt is found torqued to 22 in-lbs against a specified value of 35 ±3 in-lbs. No other symptoms are observed. Write the Problem Description and the As-Run vs. Expected fields for this DR, then recommend a disposition.
MODEL ANSWER

Problem Description: During post-install torque verification, bracket bolt B-14 was found torqued to 22 in-lbs. Specified torque per the installation procedure is 35 ±3 in-lbs. No visible damage to the fastener, bracket, or adjacent structure was observed.

Recommended Disposition: Rework. The fastener and joint are undamaged, so this is not a Repair or Scrap situation. Since the fix is simply re-torquing to the originally specified value — not an accepted deviation from it — this is Rework, not Use-As-Is: re-torque to 35 ±3 in-lbs and re-verify with a witness mark.

  • Did you state both the observed value and the specified value?
  • Does your disposition match what the fastener and structure actually need -- not just the easiest option?

Scenario — Choosing the Right Disposition

Read the situation, then choose the correct disposition.
A connector pin is found slightly bent during inspection. A technician carefully re-forms it using an approved pin tool, and it then passes a go/no-go gauge check to the original specification. What is the correct disposition?
KEY POINT

The disposition is not a formality — it is the permanent record of exactly what happened to this specific piece of flight hardware and why it was accepted. Anyone reviewing the as-built configuration later, including at PSR (Lesson 2.8), relies on that record being accurate.
