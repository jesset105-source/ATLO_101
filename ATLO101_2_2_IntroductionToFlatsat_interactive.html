Introduction to Flatsat

Before a single command, procedure, or line of flight software ever touches the actual spacecraft, it runs on flatsat first — a bench full of engineering-model avionics that lets you find out what breaks without risking anything that will fly.

What Flatsat Is

Flatsat is a "flat" (non-flight-form) assembly of engineering-model (EM) avionics — the flight computer, power distribution, RF, and other electronics boxes — wired together on a bench or in a rack instead of bolted into a spacecraft structure. It runs the same flight software, is commanded through the same ground system, and receives the same procedures the flight vehicle will — the difference is the hardware underneath is not flight hardware, and there is no flight structure, thermal environment, or mechanical integration to go with it.

That distinction matters: flatsat is not "practice testing" in some watered-down sense. It is real verification, run against real flight software and real procedures, on hardware you are allowed to get wrong.

Why Flatsat Exists

Every hour a defect spends undiscovered gets more expensive to fix the further it travels through the campaign. A procedure bug caught on flatsat is a five-minute edit. The same bug caught on the flight vehicle is a hold, a Discrepancy Report, a review cycle, and lost schedule — exactly the kind of schedule slip driver you learned about in Lesson 1.3. Flatsat exists to move that discovery as early and as cheap as possible.

KEY POINT

Every CSTM and ISTM exercise in this lesson and the next is first practiced on flatsat, exactly like every procedure is. The connector, the meter, and the pass/fail logic are real — the consequence of a mistake is not, which is exactly why this is where you learn it.
