Functional Testing

Every success criterion you wrote in Lesson 1.5 gets used for real here — the vehicle is exercised end to end, and every result gets judged against a value someone decided on before the test ever ran.

What Functional Testing Verifies

A functional test exercises a subsystem, or the whole vehicle, against the documented pass/fail criteria written during test planning (Lesson 1.5). It answers one question: does the system do what it is supposed to do, end to end, right now? It does not test survival of launch or space environments — that is what environmental testing (Lessons 2.6–2.7) is for.

Comprehensive vs. Limited Performance Tests

Not every functional test is the same depth. A Comprehensive Performance Test (CPT) exercises every documented capability of the system in detail, and is run at key campaign milestones: after integration, before and after each environmental test, and before shipment. A Limited Performance Test (LPT) is a shorter, targeted subset run more frequently — a health check, not a full re-verification — used when a full CPT would cost more schedule than the situation calls for.

The Comparison That Matters Most: Baseline vs. Current

A CPT result is not just checked against a fixed spec number — it is also compared against that same unit's own earlier CPT results. A reading can be perfectly within absolute spec and still represent a real, unexplained change from how that exact unit behaved before its last environmental test. "Passes spec" and "hasn't changed" are two different questions, and a good ATLO engineer checks both.
