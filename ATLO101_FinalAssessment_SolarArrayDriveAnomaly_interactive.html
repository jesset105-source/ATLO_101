Solar Array Drive Anomaly

Every subsystem you investigate in this assessment will report back that its own hardware is healthy. All four of them will be right. Finding out why the motor kept running anyway is going to take every one of them, together.

What You're Being Graded On

This assessment integrates everything from Foundation through PSR into one investigation: reading telemetry and schematics (Lesson 2.1), understanding CSTM/ISTM verification (2.2, 2.3), evaluating functional test data against baseline (2.4), and writing a complete, correctly-dispositioned Discrepancy Report (2.5). You will pick one subsystem team to investigate hands-on, review what the other three teams found, determine the actual root cause, and write the DR yourself.

Phase 1 — The Anomaly

During a flatsat deployment test of the Solar Array Drive Assembly (SADA), the inhibit limit switch first registered TRIPPED in telemetry at T+42.3s, indicating the array had reached full deployment and the drive motor should stop. The drive motor continued actuating for an additional 3.8 seconds before the Test Conductor called a hold per the procedure's abort criteria.

An Immediate Containment Action has already been taken: the motor circuit was safed via the GSE emergency cutoff, and no hardware damage was observed on initial inspection. A Discrepancy Report has been opened. Your job is to investigate it.

Phase 3 — Investigate

Review the evidence available to your team, then record your finding.

Structures Evidence

The inhibit switch is a mechanical microswitch mounted on the array root hinge, rated for 10 million actuation cycles. Post-anomaly inspection shows: no visible physical damage, mounting torque and witness marks intact, no FOD found in the mechanism (Lesson 2.3). One additional fact: this exact flatsat unit completed a random vibration test 3 days before this deployment test, at levels within its qualification spec (Lesson 2.6/2.7).

Structures Finding

Draft your team's finding, then reveal a model answer to self-check.
Based on the inspection, is the switch itself mechanically damaged or out of tolerance? What should Structures report to the other teams?
MODEL ANSWER

No mechanical damage found; the switch meets its physical specification and shows no signs of wear or contamination. However, Structures should still report the recent vibration exposure to the team — vibration is a known cause of transient electrical contact issues in mechanical switches even when no permanent physical damage results. A clean inspection does not rule out a vibration-induced electrical effect.

  • Did you avoid concluding "no problem, nothing to report" -- is there a fact worth flagging even without damage?

EPS Evidence

The motor driver relay coil is specified at approximately 85 Ω ±10% (76.5–93.5 Ω) at the nominal 28 VDC bus. Post-anomaly CSTM-style verification measured the relay coil at 84 Ω. Telemetry confirms bus voltage at the motor driver connector was a steady 28.1 VDC throughout the entire event, with no dropouts or transients recorded.

EPS Finding

Draft your team's finding, then reveal a model answer to self-check.
Is the EPS power/motor-driver circuit implicated in this anomaly? What should EPS report to the other teams?
MODEL ANSWER

The relay coil resistance (84 Ω) is within its 76.5–93.5 Ω spec band, and bus voltage was confirmed present and stable throughout the anomaly. EPS reports the power path is healthy and rules out an EPS hardware cause. Whatever kept the motor running, it was not a loss of power or a driver circuit fault.

  • Did you use the actual spec band (76.5-93.5 Ω), not just eyeball "84 is close to 85"?

GNC / Telemetry Finding

Draft your team's finding, then reveal a model answer to self-check.
What does the raw switch signal actually look like, and how does that compare to what you'd expect from a healthy mechanical switch making a single, clean transition?
MODEL ANSWER

The raw signal does not make one clean transition from 0 to 1. It bounces repeatedly between TRIPPED and not-TRIPPED for several seconds before finally settling. A healthy switch making a single clean mechanical contact would not produce this pattern. GNC reports the raw signal is chattering, not transitioning cleanly — this is a data point the other teams need, especially C&DH/FSW.

  • Did you describe the bouncing/chattering pattern specifically, not just "it looks weird"?

C&DH / FSW Evidence

The flight software's motor-cutoff logic works like this: the inhibit switch must report a continuously TRIPPED state for 500 milliseconds before the software commands motor cutoff. This debounce filter exists specifically to reject brief electrical noise so a single glitch can't trigger a false cutoff.

C&DH / FSW Finding

Draft your team's finding, then reveal a model answer to self-check.
If the raw switch signal were chattering on and off for several seconds (as GNC's telemetry shows), what would this 500ms debounce logic actually do, and why might the motor keep running?
MODEL ANSWER

Every time the raw signal drops back to not-TRIPPED, the 500ms debounce timer resets to zero. If the signal keeps bouncing, the software never accumulates 500ms of continuously stable TRIPPED signal, so cutoff is never commanded — even though the switch technically "tripped" many times. The software followed its logic correctly. The logic's assumption — that a switch transition is clean — is what did not hold here.

  • Did you explain WHY the timer resets, not just that it "doesn't work"?

Phase 5 — Root Cause and Discrepancy Report

You now have all four findings. Determine the actual root cause, then write the Discrepancy Report yourself, using the same format from Lesson 2.5.

What Is the Actual Root Cause?

Choose the finding that is actually supported by all four teams' evidence together.
Structures found no mechanical damage (but flagged recent vibration exposure). EPS found the power path healthy. GNC found the raw switch signal chattering instead of transitioning cleanly. C&DH/FSW found their 500ms debounce logic behaving exactly as designed. What caused the motor to keep running for 3.8 seconds?

Write the Discrepancy Report

Use the same DR format from Lesson 2.5. Draft each field, then reveal a model answer to self-check.
Write a complete DR for this anomaly: Problem Description, As-Run vs. Expected, Immediate Containment Action already taken, Root Cause, and Recommended Disposition.
MODEL ANSWER

Problem Description / As-Run vs. Expected: During flatsat SADA deployment, the inhibit switch first registered TRIPPED at T+42.3s. Per design, the drive motor should cut off within 500ms of a stable TRIPPED state. The motor instead continued actuating for 3.8 additional seconds before Test Conductor hold.

Immediate Containment Action: Motor circuit safed via GSE emergency cutoff; no hardware damage observed on initial inspection.

Root Cause: The inhibit switch produced electrical contact chatter (intermittent TRIPPED/not-TRIPPED transitions) rather than a single clean transition, likely aggravated by a random vibration test performed 3 days prior. The flight software's 500ms debounce filter, while functioning exactly as designed, reset each time the signal bounced and never accumulated 500ms of continuously stable signal until the chatter settled on its own at T+46.1s. This is a system-level interaction between marginal-but-within-spec switch behavior and a debounce threshold not tolerant of that behavior — not a failure of any single component.

Recommended Disposition: Rework. Replacing the switch alone would not fix this if the new switch also chatters under vibration. Recommend re-evaluating the debounce threshold and/or switch mounting to ensure margin against contact chatter following vibration exposure, then re-verifying with a repeat vibration-then-deployment test before this configuration is accepted for flight.

  • Does your root cause name the interaction (chatter + debounce threshold), not just "software bug" or "switch failure"?
  • Does your disposition address the actual cause -- would simply replacing the switch fix this?
ASSESSMENT COMPLETE

Notice that every individual CSTM/ISTM-style check in this investigation passed: the switch was mechanically fine, the power path was in spec, and the software followed its logic exactly as written. The anomaly only existed at the system level, in how those individually-healthy pieces interacted — which is exactly why ATLO verification never stops at the component level, and why this investigation needed all four teams to solve.
