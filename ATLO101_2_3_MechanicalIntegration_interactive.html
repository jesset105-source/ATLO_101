Mechanical Integration

Electrically verified components now have to become one physical vehicle — bolted, aligned, and torqued together in a cleanroom, by hand, under your direction.

What Mechanical Integration Involves

Once a component has passed its CSTM check, it moves from "verified electrically healthy" to "physically installed." That work is done by trained Integration Technicians (Lesson 1.2) under the direction of the ATLO engineer, inside a controlled cleanroom environment. It includes precision alignment, torquing fasteners to exact specified values in a specified sequence, and applying witness marks that prove the work was done correctly — all before the component is ever electrically powered.

Torque Control

Every fastener on a spacecraft has a specified torque value and, for multi-bolt patterns, a specified tightening sequence — typically a star or cross pattern that pulls a component down evenly rather than tilting it against an increasingly tight single corner. Under-torque risks a fastener backing out under launch vibration; over-torque risks damaging threads or warping a thin structural panel. Torque wrenches used for this work are themselves calibrated on a tracked schedule — the same "the tool has to be trustworthy before the measurement is" principle you saw with ohmmeters in Lesson 2.1.

After final torque, a witness mark (a small painted line, or "torque seal," across the fastener head and onto the adjacent structure) is applied. It doesn't add strength — it lets any later inspector see at a glance, without re-torquing anything, whether a fastener has moved since it was last verified.

FOD Control

Foreign Object Debris (FOD) — a dropped washer, a stray wire clipping, a tool left inside a structure — is treated as seriously as any electrical anomaly. A loose fastener inside a closed structure can float freely once the vehicle reaches microgravity and short a circuit or jam a mechanism years after everyone has forgotten it was ever dropped. Real cleanroom discipline includes tool accountability (every tool has a home, and every tool is accounted for at the end of a task, not just when convenient), lint-free garments, and a hard rule: nothing gets closed out until every tool and every fastener is accounted for.

Scenario — A Fastener That Won't Start Easily

Read the situation, then choose what you do.
A technician is torquing a bracket into place following the specified star pattern. On the third fastener, it takes noticeably more force than the first two just to get it started by hand, before any torque wrench is even applied. What should happen next?
KEY POINT

Nothing about mechanical integration is "just tightening bolts." Torque sequence, torque value, witness marks, and FOD accountability all exist because a mechanical defect introduced here can stay hidden until launch loads or on-orbit conditions find it — long after anyone could still fix it by hand.
