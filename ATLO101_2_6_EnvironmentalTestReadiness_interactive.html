Environmental Test Readiness

The vehicle works. Now it has to prove it keeps working after launch loads and the thermal extremes of space — and before any of that testing starts, a formal review decides whether the vehicle is actually ready for it.

What Environmental Testing Proves

Functional testing (Lesson 2.4) proves the vehicle works under comfortable, controlled conditions. Environmental testing proves it keeps working after being shaken by launch vibration, subjected to launch acoustics, and cycled through the thermal extremes and vacuum of space. A vehicle that passes every functional test but has never been environmentally tested has not actually been proven flightworthy.

GEVS-7000 Awareness

The General Environmental Verification Standard (GEVS-7000) defines the environmental test types, test levels, and sequencing used for spacecraft acceptance and qualification — it is the reason environmental testing on this program follows a standard, predictable structure instead of an ad hoc one. As with NPR 7120.5 in earlier lessons, you are not expected to memorize GEVS-7000's contents — only to know it exists, know roughly what it governs, and recognize when a test decision is being made in reference to it.

Scenario — An Unrelated Open DR Before TRR

Read the situation, then decide.
A Test Readiness Review is scheduled tomorrow for a thermal vacuum test. One Discrepancy Report remains open, written against a subsystem that will not be electrically powered or thermally cycled during this particular test. Does this block TRR?
KEY POINT

A TRR is not a formality that gets rubber-stamped whenever the schedule says it's time. It exists so that every open question about the vehicle's readiness — DRs, procedures, GSE, configuration — gets explicitly reviewed and either cleared or formally accepted, never silently ignored.
