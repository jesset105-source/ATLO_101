Pre-Shipment Review (PSR)

Every lesson in this module has been building toward this gate. PSR is where the program formally decides the vehicle is done — ready to leave ATLO for good.

What PSR Is

Pre-Shipment Review is the formal milestone review, governed under the same NPR 7120.5 framework introduced in Lesson 1.1, where the program certifies the vehicle ready to ship. It is not a re-test — it is a structured review of everything already done: every test complete and its data reviewed, every DR dispositioned or explicitly waived with rationale, the as-built configuration matching its official record, and shipping and handling arrangements ready.

Scenario — One Open Item at PSR

Read the board's finding, then make the call.
Every closure criterion is complete except one: a single DR remains open, but it has already been formally reviewed and waived, with documented rationale signed by the Systems Engineer and QA, explaining why it does not affect flight readiness. Is this a GO or a NO-GO for PSR?
GO/NO-GO

PSR does not require zero open items. It requires zero ungoverned ones. An open DR with a documented, approved waiver is a closed loop; an open DR with no disposition at all is not — that distinction is what a PSR board is actually there to check.

What Happens After PSR

A GO at PSR clears the vehicle for shipment — to a launch site, to a integration facility, wherever the campaign requires next. From here, the vehicle leaves the ATLO team's direct daily hands-on work. That is exactly why every closure criterion in this lesson exists: once this review passes, there is no more convenient opportunity to catch something before the vehicle moves on toward launch.
